## Alterations

### 1 · Healing → Temporary Hit Points `High`

Any time a Spell, Class ability, Magic Item or Spell-like effect would allow a creature to regain hit points, it instead gives them **Temporary Hit Points**. These Temporary Hit Points go away after 10 minutes.

---

### 2 · Short Rests `High`

Short Rests are **8 hours minimum**. You do not need to sleep to gain the benefit of a short rest. Level of activity can vary during a short rest, however the expectation is for characters seeking a short rest to perform actions that they would find relaxing.

In addition to getting all the benefits of a short rest a character can choose a **short rest activity** to gain additional benefits.

If a character wants to perform a short rest activity *and* sleep, the short rest will be longer — around 10 to 16 hours.

---

### 3 · Long Rests `High`

Long Rests are now **7 Days**. Long rests can only be taken in locations that offer sufficient resources to placate the stresses of adventuring.

In addition to getting all the benefits of a short rest, a character can choose a **long rest activity** to gain additional benefits.

---

### 13 · New Skill Proficiencies `High`

A few new skill proficiencies are added. These can be taken as normal skill proficiencies. Additionally, each player gets **1 extra skill proficiency** selectable only from this list.

| Skill | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Language** | Knowledge of languages, their origin and connections |
| **Culture** | Local customs, nobility etiquette, social standings, and courtly matters |
| **Money** | Market value, price negotiations, anything to do with the value of goods/services or mercantilism |

> Whenever you are able to choose a skill proficiency you may also choose from this list.

## Spell Casting

### 4 · Spell List Alterations `Low`

A list of buffed, altered, and restricted spells is included in this doc, along with reasoning. If a feature grants you a spell on this list you can instead choose a spell from the **same school of equal level or lower** to cast.

---

### 5 · One Spell Slot Per Turn `Low`

You can only expend **1 spell slot per turn**. You may cast 2 or more spells if no more than one of the spells expends a spell slot on that turn.

---

### 7 · Verbal Components Are Obvious `Low`

All Verbal components to spells are **loud, obviously magical, and impossible to conceal**. Magic by its nature is identifiable — usually visually and audibly.

Spells that target an area in a range around the caster usually draw a visible line from the target location back to the caster (e.g. Fireball). Spells of this manner can be **intercepted**.

---

### 8 · No Spells Above 6th Level `Low`

Spells 7th level and above do not exist. You can acquire spell slots as normal.

> This is more a "they don't exist in lore" rule than a mechanical cap. Players will not reach a level where 7th level slots are relevant.

---

## New Mechanics

### 9 · Health / Adrenaline / Meat `High`

Hit points are split into pools:

- **Meat Health** — equal to Constitution Modifier × Player Level
- **Adrenaline** — when you roll initiative, roll your remaining hit dice and add any additional health modifiers. This pool is your combat health for that encounter.

---

### 11 · Flashback Points `High`

The DM will not directly track any party resource — food, arrows, tools, etc. Instead the party has a **Flashback Points** pool.

- Pool maximum: **6**. Starts at 6.
- When a player wishes to use an item not written on their sheet, they can expend a Flashback Point and have a brief flashback to a reasonable past moment where they would have acquired it.
- They must still resolve the relevant resource check:
  - **Failure:** the action is expended, but the Flashback Point is **not** consumed.
  - **Success:** 1 Flashback Point is expended and the item is available.

> With good storytelling these can be used similarly to Blades in the Dark flashbacks — not limited to items.

---

### 12 · Resource Level replaces Currency `Low`

In-world currency is complicated — coins vary by country, territory, mint date, and are rarely seen above copper by common folk. To avoid this for players, each player has a **Resource Level** (slider 0–10).

| Level | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 0 | Dirt poor, nothing to your name |
| 10 | As rich as possible |

**Rules:**
- Items at a resource level *below* the player's level can be acquired in reasonable quantity without a check.
- Items *above* the player's level cannot be obtained.
- Items *equal to* the player's level, or a large sum of a lower-level item, require a **Money check**:
  - **Success:** item acquired, Resource Level drops by 1.
  - **Failure:** DM describes a worse outcome (may still include acquiring the item at a heavier cost).

> NPCs and players can acquire items above their resource level through loans or favors. A diminished Resource Level can be restored by performing adequate trade or plying a skill that brings in that level of income — typically over a Long Rest.

---

### 15 · Morale Saves `Low`

Morale saves are saves **enemies and NPCs** must make at the end of their turns when they are at **half or fewer HP**.

---

## Tweaks

### 6 · Retrieving Bagged Items `Low`

Items in a bag that are not declared to be sheathed, holstered, or otherwise carried for easy use take **12 seconds to 1 minute** to retrieve.

Flashback Points can be expended to declare that such items would have been readily accessible, if their placement is not obvious or previously stated.

---

### 10 · Celestial = Draconic `Low`

Due to worldbuilding, any Spell, Magic Item, or Class Ability that specifies a restriction or pool of targets including the **Celestial** keyword now also includes the **Draconic** keyword.

---

### 14 · Masterwork Items `Low`

Masterwork is a non-magical item modifier from older editions, now included in this system. A Masterwork item is created by a Master Crafter — it represents the **best possible version** of that item.

- Most magical items are created using a masterwork base item.
- Masterwork items cost **1 resource level above** their regular counterpart.
- Masterwork items **always outperform** their mundane equivalents.
